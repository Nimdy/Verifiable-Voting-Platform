# Threat Model

> Every role is assumed potentially malicious. Integrity must survive any single dishonest insider and produce **public, detectable** evidence of cheating.

## Assets to protect

- Voter intent / cast-as-intended fidelity (the choice the human actually made vs. what gets encrypted on their endpoint)
- Ballot secrecy / the link between a voter's identity and their plaintext choice (must never be recoverable)
- Encrypted ballots + their disjunctive Chaum-Pedersen validity proofs (the recorded-as-cast artifacts)
- Trustee secret key shares (Pedersen DKG x_i; per .gitignore trustee-shares/ and *.key must never be committed)
- The joint election public key h = combine(trustee pubs) and the trustee public commitments
- Tally integrity / counted-as-recorded (the homomorphic aggregate + threshold decryption shares + decryption correctness proofs + the final claimed tally)
- Bulletin-board append-only log: entry set, ordering, signed Merkle roots (RFC 6962 STH), inclusion + consistency proofs
- Eligibility roll / electoral register and the off-ledger identity-proofing data (kept off-ledger by ADR-1; never SSN/government-ID-derived)
- Per-election voter credentials (Belenios-style Ed25519/Schnorr keypairs) and the issuance-to-casting unlinkability
- Spent-nullifier set (single-use enforcement; published per-credential nullifiers, NOT TTL)
- The ElectionManifest (Zod-validated config: adapters, parameters, trustee set, contest definitions, snapshot block, voting-power strategy)
- The published transcript (everything the world re-verifies: pubkey, trustee pubs, ballots+proofs, board root, aggregate, decryption shares+proofs, claimed tally)
- The Rust->WASM crypto core binary delivered to the voter's browser, and the independent Python verifier
- Reproducible-build provenance / signed release artifacts and the software supply chain (npm @noble/*, Cargo crates, circom/arkworks, Foundry)
- Fiat-Shamir / Merlin-STROBE transcript domain separation and randomness (the entropy used for r, DKG, nonces)
- Voter-verified paper ballots of record + chain-of-custody (polling-place profile only; the sole software-independence anchor)
- Anchor adapter targets and their signing keys (EVM/Cosmos-BFT/Bitcoin roots), and governance settlement contracts (oSnap/SafeSnap, timelock, dispute window)
- Voting-power snapshot (governance profile: token/NFT/quadratic/delegation/allowlist Merkle root at a frozen block)
- System availability during the open voting window (liveness of casting service, board, trustees)

## Trust assumptions

- Trust root is peer-reviewed cryptography (ElectionGuard-2.x-style exponential ElGamal over ristretto255, disjunctive Chaum-Pedersen NIZKs, Pedersen DKG, Benaloh cast-or-challenge), NOT any blockchain. The chain is explicitly non-load-bearing (ADR-2): it carries only ~32-byte signed Merkle roots, never ballots.
- The ristretto255 group is sound; the discrete-log / DDH assumptions hold; @noble/curves and the audited Rust crates correctly implement the primitives; no hand-rolled ciphers (ADR-7).
- The Fiat-Shamir transform is instantiated strongly (full statement bound into the challenge over a Merlin/STROBE transcript) so weak-FS forgeries are infeasible; SHA-256/512 behave as random oracles for this purpose.
- Integrity survives if AT LEAST ONE of {registrar, casting server} is honest (Belenios separation): a single one of them cannot both forge eligibility and alter ballots undetectably.
- Threshold privacy holds as long as fewer than the threshold k of n trustees collude; the joint secret is never reconstructed (Lagrange-combined partials only). NOTE: the current Stage-1 reference PoC is N-of-N with independent random shares and NO real Pedersen DKG, so its privacy needs ALL n honest and its liveness needs ALL n available.
- At least one honest party runs the independent verifier on the full published transcript, and at least one honest party (voter or observer) performs Benaloh challenges; verifiability is only as strong as the fraction of people who actually verify.
- The independent Python verifier is built by a different team/language than the Rust core (ADR-6) so a single implementation bug cannot mask itself; both plus the open ElectionGuard verifier agree on a transcript (M1 done-criterion).
- The bulletin board is genuinely append-only, replicated, and publicly readable; observers fetch signed tree heads and cross-check consistency, so equivocation (showing different logs to different people) is detectable by anyone who gossips roots.
- Identity proofing happens off-ledger and is NOT derived from SSN or any durable government ID (ADR-1); the voting layer sees only an unlinkable >=128-bit per-election 'is-eligible' attestation.
- Receipt-freeness is a design invariant (ADR-8): no voter is ever given a transferable cryptographic proof of how they voted; the original 'see your vote on the chain' step is deliberately removed.
- Software independence is achieved ONLY in the polling-place profile via voter-verified paper ballots of record + mandatory RLA (ADR-4); society/governance/remote profiles explicitly do NOT achieve software independence and say so plainly.
- Deployments are within declared scope: low-stakes, low-coercion, recoverable contests (clubs, societies, HOAs, DAOs/governance, community polls). Binding government/public elections are out of scope and unsupported.
- If any anchoring chain is used it is permissioned BFT with named, accountable validators, or a public chain used only to timestamp roots; Proof-of-Stake as the election authority is rejected (ADR-3).
- Operators correctly configure the ElectionManifest (trustee set, threshold, snapshot, adapters) and protect trustee key shares and any anchor/registrar signing keys per the .gitignore secret-hygiene rules.
- The current state is a Stage-1 PoC: it has NOT had external crypto review or red-teaming (those are M7); no production security claims hold until M0 threat-model sign-off and M7 review are complete.

## Adversaries

| Adversary | Capability | Goal |
|-----------|------------|------|
| Insider election official / platform admin (operator of casting service + coordinator) | Full control of the casting/coordinator servers and the default Postgres bulletin board: can attempt to alter/drop/reorder stored ballots, stuff extra ballots, equivocate on the log, mis-state the tally, manipulate the ElectionManifest, suppress entries, and shape availability. Cannot break ristretto255 or forge NIZKs/signatures. | Change the outcome (favor a candidate/proposal) or deanonymize voters while appearing to run an honest election; ideally cheat without being caught. |
| Malicious trustee (one of the n DKG key-share holders) | Holds one threshold secret share x_i; can publish a wrong/garbage decryption share, refuse to participate (withhold a share), collude with up to k-1 other trustees, and try to misbehave during DKG. Cannot decrypt alone (privacy holds below threshold). | Either learn individual plaintext ballots (collude to reach threshold) or sabotage/bias the count (bad or withheld shares), preferably undetectably. |
| Compromised endpoint / malicious client (malware or hostile JS on the voter's device, or a hostile casting front-end) | Total control of what the voter sees and what gets encrypted: can flip the choice before encryption, exfiltrate the plaintext and randomness r (breaking that voter's secrecy), suppress or fake the Benaloh challenge UI, and serve a tampered WASM core. This is the dominant real-world attack surface for any digital voting system. | Cast-as-intended subversion (record a different vote than intended) and/or deanonymize the voter, ideally hidden from the voter. |
| Malicious registrar / identity provider | Issues per-election credentials and holds the eligibility roll and (off-ledger) identity-proofing data; can issue credentials to ineligible/phantom voters, withhold credentials from eligible voters (disenfranchise), or attempt to link issued credentials back to casting events. Constrained by Belenios separation (cannot also alter ballots if the server is honest). | Stuff the roll with phantom voters, disenfranchise targeted real voters, or deanonymize by correlating issuance with casting. |
| Malicious bulletin-board operator | Runs the append-only log / transparency-log backend (or the object-store/IPFS mirror); can attempt to omit, reorder, or retroactively edit entries, present a split-view (equivocate) to different observers, withhold inclusion/consistency proofs, or stall publication. Cannot forge signed roots without the signing key or break Merkle binding. | Suppress or alter recorded ballots, or show different observers different ledgers, to bias the tally or hide tampering. |
| Chain validators / anchor-chain operators (only if an anchor adapter is enabled) | Control inclusion/ordering/finality of anchored Merkle roots on the chosen chain (EVM / permissioned CometBFT / Bitcoin OP_RETURN); can censor or delay a root anchoring, reorg a public-chain anchor (probabilistic finality), or (in governance/oSnap mode) attempt to manipulate settlement timing. Cannot read ballots (none are on-chain) or forge a root that doesn't match the real log. | Weaken or repudiate the tamper-evidence timestamp, censor anchoring, or in governance settlement force an incorrect on-chain outcome. |
| Network / DoS attacker | Network-position adversary: can drop/delay traffic, DoS the casting service / board / trustee nodes / verifier endpoints during the voting window, partition observers from each other, and attempt downgrade/MITM on transport (TLS). | Deny availability (prevent casting/closing/decryption/verification), or exploit a partition to hide equivocation, biasing turnout or stalling the result. |
| Coercer / vote-buyer | Can pressure or pay a voter and demand proof of how they voted; in remote settings can stand over the voter or compel disclosure of credentials/device. Does not need to break cryptography. | Buy or coerce votes by obtaining a transferable proof of a voter's choice, or by voting on the voter's behalf. |
| Supply-chain attacker | Can attempt to compromise dependencies and build pipeline: npm packages (@noble/curves, @noble/hashes), Cargo crates, circom/arkworks circuits, Foundry tooling, the WASM build, the published voter app, or the release signing; can backdoor the crypto core or the verifier, or substitute a malicious WASM bundle in transit. | Insert a covert backdoor (weak randomness, key exfiltration, biased proofs, a verifier that rubber-stamps fraud) that survives into voters' browsers and observers' verifiers. |

## Threats & mitigations

| # | Actor | Threat | Impact | Mitigation | Residual risk |
|---|-------|--------|--------|------------|---------------|
| T1 | Insider election official / platform admin | Admin alters, deletes, or reorders an already-recorded encrypted ballot on the Postgres board to change the count (demonstrated as Insider Attack A in reference/src/demo.ts, which mutates voter-3's ciphertext b by adding the public key). | Outcome manipulation; loss of recorded-as-cast / counted-as-recorded integrity. | Every published ballot is committed by the RFC-6962 Merkle root over canonical bytes (reference/src/bulletin.ts + codec.ts); any mutation changes the root AND breaks the disjunctive Chaum-Pedersen validity proof. The independent verifier (reference/src/verify.ts checks 2 and 3) recomputes the root from the published ballots and re-verifies every proof, so tampering is caught by anyone re-verifying the transcript. | Tamper-EVIDENCE, not tamper-prevention: detection requires that someone actually re-verifies and that observers have a trustworthy copy of the signed root to compare against (see T6 equivocation). A tamper that nobody verifies is not 'caught.' Recoverability assumes the contest can be re-run, which holds only in the declared low-stakes scope. |
| T2 | Insider election official / platform admin | Admin stuffs extra ballots or injects an out-of-range value (e.g. encrypts '10' and claims it is a '1'), as in Insider Attack B in demo.ts. | Inflated/biased tally; ballot stuffing. | Each ciphertext must carry a disjunctive Chaum-Pedersen proof that it encrypts exactly 0 or 1 (proveBit/verifyBit in reference/src/proofs.ts); 2000/2000 randomized soundness tests confirm forged proofs for out-of-range values are rejected. In the full design, eligibility-bounded stuffing is additionally constrained by Belenios per-credential signatures plus the published spent-nullifier set, so only one valid ballot per credential counts. | The Stage-1 PoC has NO credential or nullifier layer: it proves each ballot is a valid 0/1 but does NOT bind ballots to eligible voters or cap one-per-voter, so an admin can still append unlimited well-formed phantom ballots. This is only closed once M3 (registrar + nullifiers) lands AND observers can independently reconcile the ballot count against the published eligibility roll size. A dishonest registrar+server together can still inflate the eligible set (see T9, mitigated only by Belenios one-honest-party separation). |
| T3 | Insider election official / platform admin | Corrupt authority announces a fabricated tally (e.g. claims an all-YES landslide), as in Insider Attack C in demo.ts. | Result falsification. | The tally is not trusted: the verifier homomorphically re-aggregates all published ballots, requires every trustee decryption share to carry a valid Chaum-Pedersen correctness proof, recombines the shares, and recomputes the discrete log itself (verify.ts checks 4-6). A claimed tally that does not equal the independently decrypted aggregate is rejected. | Soundness of this check rests on the trustee threshold assumption (T4) and on DDH/discrete-log holding. If the adversary controls enough trustees to produce a consistent-but-dishonest decryption, the proofs would pass; the homomorphic re-tally only binds the count to whatever ballot set is on the board, so it must be paired with T1/T2 mitigations. |
| T4 | Malicious trustee | A trustee publishes a wrong decryption share to corrupt the count, withholds its share to block decryption, or colludes with others to reach the threshold and decrypt individual ballots / the running tally. | Tally corruption, denial of result (liveness), or loss of ballot secrecy if a threshold colludes. | Each share carries a Chaum-Pedersen proof that log_g(pub_i) == log_a(share_i) (proveDecryption/verifyDecryption); a wrong share cannot produce a valid proof (selftest case 5). The design uses Pedersen k-of-n DKG so the secret is never reconstructed and up to k-1 corrupt trustees learn nothing and cannot complete a dishonest decryption; k honest trustees suffice for liveness. The PoC also demonstrates a lone trustee cannot decrypt a single ballot (singleTrusteeAttempt in election.ts). | Privacy collapses if >= k trustees collude (an inherent threshold assumption, not a bug). CRITICAL GAP: the Stage-1 PoC implements N-of-N with independent random shares and NO Pedersen DKG, so today privacy requires ALL n honest and liveness requires ALL n available -- a single withholding/faulty trustee currently DoSes the result and there is no robustness. Real DKG with verifiable share complaints (M3) is required before any of the threshold robustness claims hold. |
| T5 | Compromised endpoint / malicious client | Malware or hostile JavaScript on the voter's device flips the choice before encryption (shows 'Yes', encrypts 'No'), exfiltrates the plaintext and randomness r, or serves a tampered WASM core; a hostile front-end fakes the Benaloh challenge UI. | Cast-as-intended subversion and/or deanonymization of that voter; the dominant real-world voting attack surface. | Benaloh cast-or-challenge lets a voter spoil a ballot and audit it on an INDEPENDENT device, giving a probabilistic guarantee that the encryption matched intent before committing; the verifier runs in-browser via WASM so a malicious server cannot fake the audit result. In the polling-place profile, voter-verified PAPER ballots of record + RLA (T13) provide software independence that defeats endpoint flips entirely. | This is the platform's most honest limitation. For remote/digital-only profiles (governance, society remote), a compromised endpoint that controls BOTH the casting UI and the challenge UI on the same device can largely defeat Benaloh against an unsophisticated voter, and exfiltration of (m, r) breaks that voter's secrecy outright. Cast-as-intended on a malware endpoint is achievable ONLY with a second trusted device or paper; the README states plainly that remote profiles do NOT achieve software independence. Most of this threat is accepted/out-of-scope (see out-of-scope list). |
| T6 | Malicious bulletin-board operator | The log operator equivocates -- presents a 'split view', showing different ballot sets / different signed roots to different observers (e.g. one log to voters checking inclusion, another to the verifier), or silently omits/withholds inclusion and consistency proofs. | Defeats recorded-as-cast and the entire tamper-evidence model if undetected, because each victim sees a locally-consistent but globally-divergent ledger. | RFC-6962 transparency-log semantics: signed tree heads + Merkle consistency proofs allow any observer to verify the log is append-only, and cross-observer GOSSIP of signed roots detects equivocation. Optional anchoring writes the signed root to one or more independent targets (Postgres + IPFS + permissioned BFT chain in parallel, per M6) so a single operator cannot present divergent roots to all of them without detection. | Equivocation is only DETECTED, not prevented, and detection depends on a functioning gossip/monitor ecosystem that does not yet exist for a self-hosted instance; a lone voter who never compares their root with anyone else can be shown a private fake. The Stage-1 board is in-memory with no signed roots, monitors, or consistency proofs at all (M2 work). Network partition (T7) can suppress the gossip that would reveal a split view. |
| T7 | Network / DoS attacker | Attacker DoSes the casting service, board, trustee nodes, or verifier endpoints during the voting window, or partitions observers to prevent root gossip; attempts TLS downgrade/MITM on transport. | Denial of availability (cannot cast / close / decrypt / verify), or a partition that masks board equivocation (T6); selective censorship can bias turnout. | Architecture favors availability resilience: replicated append-only board, multiple parallel anchor targets, object-store/IPFS mirrors, and an asynchronous verify-anytime model (verification is done from the published transcript after the fact, not in real time). Standard TLS + signed artifacts prevent MITM tampering (a MITM cannot forge signed roots or valid proofs). | Availability is not cryptographically guaranteed: a determined DoS can prevent timely casting or stall decryption, and the N-of-N PoC trustee model (T4) makes liveness brittle. Selective censorship of specific voters' submissions is hard to distinguish from voluntary abstention without independent receipt-tracking. DoS-induced disenfranchisement is bounded by the low-stakes / recoverable scope (re-run the poll), not eliminated. |
| T8 | Coercer / vote-buyer | A coercer or buyer demands that a voter prove how they voted, or compels the voter's credential/device, in order to pay for or pressure a specific vote. | Vote-buying and coercion; subversion of free choice. | Receipt-freeness by design (ADR-8): no voter ever receives a transferable proof of their choice -- only encrypted ballots are published and only aggregate tallies are released, so a tracker reveals nothing about the choice. This is exactly why the original 'see your vote on the chain' step was removed. Optional coercion-RESISTANT tiers (Selene deferred fakeable trackers, MACI/Semaphore anti-collusion, full JCJ/Civitas anonymous credentials) let a coerced voter produce a convincing FAKE proof or override a coerced vote. | Base profiles deliver receipt-freeness but NOT coercion-resistance: a coercer who watches the voter cast in real time (over-the-shoulder, or compels the device/credential) still succeeds, since the voter genuinely casts under duress. Strong coercion-resistance requires the optional tiers (JCJ/Civitas additionally assumes an UNTAPPABLE registration channel and pays a quadratic tally cost; MACI relies on a trusted/non-colluding coordinator). These tiers are optional, advanced (M6), and unimplemented in Stage-1; for the declared low-coercion scope this residual is acceptable but real. |
| T9 | Malicious registrar / identity provider | Registrar issues credentials to phantom/ineligible voters, withholds credentials from eligible voters to disenfranchise them, or correlates credential issuance (time/order/device) with casting to deanonymize voters. | Roll stuffing, targeted disenfranchisement, or breach of ballot secrecy via issuance-to-casting linkage. | Belenios separation of duties: integrity survives if EITHER the registrar OR the server is honest -- a dishonest registrar alone cannot also alter recorded ballots. Issuance is decoupled from casting (time/order/device) and credentials are unlinkable >=128-bit per-election values; identity proofing is off-ledger and never SSN/government-ID-derived (ADR-1), so the voting layer sees only an 'is-eligible' attestation. Observers reconcile the published nullifier/ballot count against the attested eligible-roll size. | If the registrar AND the server collude, both phantom issuance and ballot insertion become possible (the Belenios one-honest-party assumption is exactly that -- an assumption). Disenfranchisement by silently withholding credentials is hard to prove externally without an independent eligibility audit. Issuance-casting unlinkability depends on disciplined operational separation that a self-hoster can misconfigure. None of registrar/credentials/nullifiers exist in Stage-1 (M3 work). |
| T10 | Supply-chain attacker | Attacker backdoors a dependency or build artifact -- npm @noble/curves\|@noble/hashes, a Cargo crate, a circom/arkworks circuit, the WASM build, the released voter app, or the release signing -- e.g. weakening randomness for r/DKG, exfiltrating keys, biasing proofs, or making the verifier rubber-stamp fraud. | Total, covert compromise: a backdoored core or verifier can break secrecy or integrity for every election it touches while passing all checks. | Defense in depth by diversity: the security-critical core is Rust->WASM using only audited crates (no hand-rolled ciphers, ADR-7), and the INDEPENDENT verifier is written in a different language by a different team (ADR-6) AND cross-checked against the open ElectionGuard verifier (M1 done-criterion), so a backdoor would have to compromise multiple independent toolchains identically to stay hidden. M7 mandates external review, red-teaming, and signed reproducible releases; transcripts are publicly re-verifiable with independent tooling. | A SHARED dependency used by both the core and the verifier (or a compromise of the reproducible-build/signing infrastructure itself) defeats the diversity argument. The browser delivery path (a swapped WASM bundle served to the voter, related to T5) is a live risk for remote profiles even with reproducible builds, since most voters will not verify the WASM hash. Reproducible builds + signing + multi-implementation agreement reduce but cannot fully eliminate trusting-trust risk; none of the review/reproducible-release machinery exists yet (pre-M7). |
| T11 | Chain validators / anchor-chain operators | When an anchor adapter is enabled, validators censor/delay anchoring of a signed root, reorg a public-chain anchor (probabilistic finality), or in governance/oSnap settlement attempt to force an incorrect on-chain outcome or grief the dispute window. | Weakened or repudiable tamper-evidence timestamp; in governance mode, an incorrect trustless settlement. | The chain is non-load-bearing by ADR-2: it carries ONLY ~32-byte signed Merkle roots, never ballots, so a hostile chain cannot read votes or alter the actual log; the real bulletin board (Postgres/object-store) remains the source of truth and roots can be anchored to several targets in parallel (M6). PoS-as-authority is rejected (ADR-3); any chain must be permissioned BFT with named, accountable validators or a public chain used only for timestamping. Governance settlement (oSnap/SafeSnap) sits behind a dispute window + timelock so an incorrect proposal can be challenged before execution. | Anchor CENSORSHIP/delay can deny the independent timestamp (but not corrupt the off-chain truth). Probabilistic-finality public chains (Bitcoin/EVM) give a weaker 'immutability' than the marketing word implies; this is why anchoring is optional and belt-and-suspenders. In governance settlement, the correctness of the on-chain outcome still depends on the dispute window being watched by an honest challenger; an unwatched window can settle a wrong result. |
| T12 | Insider election official / platform admin | Cryptographic protocol-misuse / mis-binding: re-using a valid ballot+proof across contests or voters, weak Fiat-Shamir (challenge not binding the full statement), nonce/randomness reuse in r or DKG, or ElectionManifest manipulation (swapping option labels, trustee set, or snapshot block). | Replay/duplication of ballots, forgeable proofs, secrecy loss from nonce reuse, or silent misconfiguration that biases the result. | Fiat-Shamir binds the full statement, not just commitments, over a Merlin/STROBE transcript (group.ts hashToScalar binds the label + public points; the design uses domain-separated STROBE), which defeats weak-FS forgeries; the soundness suite checks proof non-malleability (selftest case 3 -- any single-bit mutation breaks the proof). The ElectionManifest is Zod-typed and is part of the published transcript so any swap of options/trustees/snapshot is publicly auditable. | GAP: the Stage-1 proveBit binds only {h, a, b} and proveDecryption binds {G, a, pub, share} -- neither binds a contest ID, voter/credential, or election-instance nonce, so a well-formed ballot+proof is REPLAYABLE across contests/voters in the current PoC (no replay protection until credentials/nullifiers + per-contest domain separation land in M2/M3). Randomness quality depends on the platform RNG; manifest manipulation is only caught if observers actually diff the published manifest against the agreed parameters. |
| T13 | Compromised endpoint / malicious client (polling-place profile) | In the supervised polling-place profile, the voting/scanning device flips or mis-records votes despite Benaloh, attempting to defeat cast-as-intended at scale. | Mass cast-as-intended subversion in the one profile that targets in-person, higher-integrity use. | Software independence: the voter-verified PAPER ballot is the legal record (inspected before the electronic cast finalizes, retained behind glass), and a MANDATORY risk-limiting audit (RLA) compares the electronic tally against the paper; paper WINS on discrepancy. The M4 done-criterion is explicitly that an injected endpoint-flip is CAUGHT by the RLA against paper. This is the only configuration that achieves true software independence (ADR-4). | Operationally heavy and only as good as execution: depends on voters actually checking the paper, correct chain-of-custody, an honestly conducted RLA with adequate sample sizes, and a real recovery/recount procedure. It is opt-in and unimplemented (M4). It mitigates endpoint flips but not coercion (T8) or registrar attacks (T9). Even here the platform's own scope bars BINDING government use. |

## Explicitly out of scope

- A coerced voter operating on a malware-controlled endpoint with no second trusted device and no paper -- cast-as-intended against full endpoint compromise is the explicitly accepted limitation for all remote/digital-only profiles (NASEM 2018; Park-Specter-Narula-Rivest 2021; Voatz/USENIX 2020). The only in-architecture defense is the polling-place paper+RLA profile.
- Binding government / public elections, remote or in-person -- categorically out of scope and unsupported. The platform refuses to claim government-grade integrity without voter-verified paper ballots of record + RLA, and even the polling-place profile is gated to low-stakes, low-coercion, recoverable contests only.
- Strong coercion-resistance in the base (governance/society) profiles -- base profiles deliver receipt-freeness only; defeating an over-the-shoulder coercer or a voter compelled to hand over credentials/device requires the optional Selene/MACI/JCJ tiers, which carry their own assumptions (JCJ: untappable registration channel; MACI: non-colluding coordinator) and are not part of the default guarantee.
- Breaking the underlying cryptographic primitives -- discrete-log / DDH on ristretto255, the soundness of @noble/curves and audited Rust crates, SHA-256/512 as random oracles for Fiat-Shamir, and Ed25519/Schnorr signatures are ASSUMED sound. Cryptanalysis of these (including a future cryptographically-relevant quantum computer; the stack is not post-quantum) is out of scope.
- Trustee collusion at or above the threshold k -- privacy loss when >= k of n trustees collude is an inherent, accepted property of threshold decryption, not a defect to be mitigated; the mitigation is governance (choosing independent, accountable trustees and a sensible k/n).
- Physical-world and procedural security outside the protocol -- ballot-box / paper chain-of-custody failures in the polling-place profile, physical compromise of trustee key-storage hardware, social engineering of operators, and legal/jurisdictional disputes are operational concerns for the deployer, not addressed by the cryptographic core.
- Voter-side opsec and human factors -- voters who never run a Benaloh challenge, never re-verify the transcript, never gossip/compare signed roots, or who reuse compromised personal devices; verifiability is only as strong as the fraction of participants who actually verify, and the platform cannot force them to.
- Long-term / 'harvest-now-decrypt-later' confidentiality of published encrypted ballots -- because encrypted ballots are public forever, any future break of the encryption (e.g. quantum) would retroactively expose individual votes; mitigating this is out of scope and is an additional reason the system is unsuitable for high-stakes secrecy-critical use.
- Availability/DoS as a hard guarantee -- the platform provides redundancy and verify-anytime resilience but makes NO cryptographic liveness guarantee; sustained DoS or selective censorship during the window is handled by the recoverability (re-run) assumption of the low-stakes scope, not prevented.
- Pre-M0/M7 production trust -- no security guarantee is claimed for the current Stage-1 PoC; external crypto review, red-teaming, and signed reproducible releases (M7) plus threat-model sign-off (M0) are prerequisites, and a real pilot is explicitly NOT a binding public election.
