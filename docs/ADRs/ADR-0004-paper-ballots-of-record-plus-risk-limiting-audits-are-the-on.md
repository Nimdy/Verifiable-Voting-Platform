# ADR-0004: Paper ballots of record plus risk-limiting audits are the only software-independence anchor; digital-only deployments must state they do not achieve software independence

**Status:** Accepted

## Context
End-to-end verifiability (cast-as-intended, recorded-as-cast, counted-as-recorded) is necessary but NOT sufficient against a compromised endpoint. Malware on the voter's device can display 'Candidate A' while encrypting 'Candidate B'; the cryptography then faithfully and verifiably carries the WRONG choice all the way to the tally. The only known defense is software independence (Rivest-Wack): an undetected change or error in software cannot cause an undetectable change in the outcome, achieved by a voter-verified paper ballot that is the legal record, checked against the electronic count by a risk-limiting audit (RLA). The reference implementation proves the cryptographic E2E-V layer (catching ballot tampering, stuffing, rigged tally in the demo) but a TypeScript/Rust core, however sound, can do NOTHING about an endpoint that lies to the voter before encryption. The original concept treated VVPAT as optional and handed the paper to the voter (Steps 18, 88-89), which both fails as an audit anchor (optional/unreconciled paper cannot bound risk) and creates a coercion receipt.

## Decision
Voter-verified paper ballots of record + a mandatory RLA are designated the ONLY mechanism in this platform that delivers software independence, and they are confined to the in-person, supervised 'polling-place' profile. In that profile: the PAPER ballot is the legal record; it is voter-verified BEFORE the electronic cast finalizes; it is retained under chain-of-custody (not handed to the voter -- see ADR-0008); a risk-limiting audit (ballot-polling or comparison, e.g. Arlo-style tooling in rla/) is MANDATORY and PAPER WINS on any discrepancy. The polling-place profile additionally requires endpoint-integrity controls (reproducible builds, measured boot, custody). All remote/digital-only profiles -- 'governance' and 'society' -- MUST state plainly, in their manifest and user-facing docs, that they DO NOT achieve software independence and rely on endpoint honesty; they are scoped to low-stakes, low-coercion, recoverable contests only. Milestone M4's definition-of-done makes this testable: an injected endpoint-flip MUST be caught by the RLA against paper.

## Consequences
GOOD: gives an honest, defensible line between what the digital-only system can and cannot promise, matching NASEM 2018 and Park et al.; the polling-place profile has a real, expert-endorsed defense against endpoint compromise; prevents the project from over-claiming and from drifting toward the binding-government use case it explicitly disclaims; the RLA provides a statistical, public, paper-anchored assurance that is independent of all the software. BAD/COSTS: heavy operational burden (printing, secure custody, audit staffing, ballot manifests, sampling) that most club/DAO/society deployments will not want and do not need; software independence is simply UNAVAILABLE to remote/online deployments -- this is a hard, stated limitation, not a roadmap item to 'fix later'; introduces physical-world processes outside the codebase's control (custody integrity, printer trust). These costs are accepted because the alternative -- implying digital-only E2E-V defeats endpoint malware -- would be dishonest and dangerous.

## Alternatives considered
(1) Claim cryptographic E2E-V alone provides software independence -- rejected: false; E2E-V verifies what was recorded, not whether the endpoint recorded the voter's true intent. (2) Benaloh cast-or-challenge as the sole endpoint defense -- valuable and used for cast-as-intended, but it is probabilistic, depends on a second independent device, and most voters will not challenge; insufficient to anchor a binding audit, so it complements rather than replaces paper+RLA. (3) Optional/unreconciled VVPAT handed to the voter (original concept) -- rejected: cannot bound outcome risk and is a coercion receipt. (4) Trusted-hardware/remote attestation to 'trust' the endpoint -- rejected as a software-independence substitute: shifts trust to opaque vendor firmware, is not voter-verifiable, and the Voatz analysis shows such endpoint trust is misplaced.
