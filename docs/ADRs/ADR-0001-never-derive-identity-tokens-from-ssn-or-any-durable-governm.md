# ADR-0001: Never derive identity tokens from SSN or any durable government identifier; keep identity proofing off-ledger

**Status:** Accepted

## Context
The 2014 seed concept (docs/ORIGINAL_CONCEPT.md) rooted eligibility in a Social Security Token (SST) derived from the voter's SSN, with downstream VET/AAVET tokens linked back to it. SSNs and analogous durable national identifiers (driver-license numbers, passport numbers, national-ID numbers) have catastrophic properties for a voting credential: (a) low entropy and structured (a 9-digit SSN has < 30 bits and large swaths are already public or guessable from area/group/serial structure), so hashing one is a trivially reversible dictionary attack -- 'hashing the SSN' provides no anonymity; (b) non-revocable -- a person cannot rotate their SSN if a per-election credential leaks; (c) globally linkable -- the same identifier across every election builds a permanent cross-election voting dossier; (d) already mass-breached. Binding any of this into a published transcript, or even into the registrar's credential-derivation function, manufactures a national-scale surveillance honeypot and de-anonymization oracle. The platform's mission is 'verify everything, reveal nothing' and to remove insider footholds; an SSN-rooted token is the single largest insider/linkage foothold conceivable.

## Decision
The voting layer MUST NOT ingest, store, hash, or derive any credential from an SSN or any durable government identifier. Identity proofing (the question 'is this person eligible') stays entirely OFF the ledger and OUT of the cryptographic core. The IdentityProvider port emits only a boolean-equivalent 'is-eligible' attestation against an organization roll / OIDC / Semaphore / JCJ-credential adapter; it never passes a government identifier into the AnonymityLayer or BulletinBoard. For each election the Registrar issues a FRESH, election-scoped credential with >= 128 bits of entropy from a CSPRNG -- in the reference and target design a Belenios-style Ed25519/Schnorr keypair (the voter signs the ciphertext; the registrar sees the public key, the casting layer sees a signature). The credential is unlinkable across elections by construction: it is randomly generated, not deterministically derived from any stable identity. The mapping from a real person to their per-election credential public key, if it must exist at all for an org roll, lives only in the Registrar's private store, is never published, and is never an input to any transcript artifact. Code-level invariant: types crossing the IdentityProvider -> AnonymityLayer boundary carry no PII field; conformance tests assert no government-ID-shaped data reaches the bulletin board.

## Consequences
GOOD: eliminates the highest-value de-anonymization and cross-election-linkage target; credentials are cheaply revocable and rotatable per election; the public transcript provably contains no PII (only group elements, proofs, and a Merkle root, as in src/election.ts Transcript); strong fit for the bottom-up target users (clubs, DAOs, societies) who already have their own rolls and have no business handling SSNs; reduces regulatory/data-protection surface (GDPR/CCPA, breach-notification) for self-hosters. BAD/COSTS: the system cannot itself answer 'is this the same legal person who voted last year' or do nationwide cross-jurisdiction de-duplication -- that is deliberately pushed to the off-ledger Registrar/IdP and its policy; Sybil-resistance now depends on the quality of the organization's roll or chosen IdP, which becomes a named trust assumption in THREAT_MODEL.md (registrar honesty); a malicious registrar could still issue credentials to ineligible voters, so integrity relies on the Belenios separation (integrity holds if registrar OR server is honest) plus published per-credential nullifiers for one-vote-per-credential.

## Alternatives considered
(1) Hash/HMAC the SSN with a per-election salt -- rejected: low entropy makes it brute-forceable, the salt does not add entropy to the underlying secret, and a colluding salt-holder reverses it. (2) Encrypt SSNs at rest in the registrar -- rejected: still concentrates a national honeypot and does nothing for the linkage/non-revocability problems. (3) Government PKI / smart-ID national identity as the credential -- rejected for the low-stakes bottom-up scope: unavailable to clubs/DAOs, reintroduces durable cross-election linkage, and ties the platform to exactly the binding-government use case this project explicitly does not serve. (4) JCJ/Civitas anonymous credentials as the default -- deferred to an optional coercion-resistant tier (ADR-0008 territory) due to quadratic tally cost and the untappable-registration assumption; the chosen default is fresh per-election Belenios credentials.
