# ADR-0010: Everlasting (post-quantum) ballot privacy via a perfectly-hiding Pedersen commitment trail bound to the verifiable ballots

**Status:** Accepted (primitive shipped; full-deployment migration deferred)

## Context
Every ballot the engine publishes is an exponential-ElGamal ciphertext over ristretto255. ElGamal privacy is **computational**: it rests on the hardness of the discrete log. Voting has an unusually long privacy horizon — a public bulletin board persists for decades — so the relevant adversary is **harvest-now, decrypt-later**: record the public transcript today, break the curve tomorrow (a cryptographically-relevant quantum computer, or any future advance), and retroactively de-anonymize every ballot ever cast. None of the engine's other layers change this: the bit proofs, the threshold-decryption transcript, and the anchored roots all assume discrete log is hard *forever*. This was the one frontier privacy risk the project had not even named — a silent gap, out of character for a kernel that documents every other limit. The established literature answer is **everlasting privacy via perfectly-(information-theoretically)-hiding commitments** (Moran–Naor; Cuvelier–Pereira–Peters, "Election verifiability or ballot privacy: do we need to choose?", ESORICS 2013 — the "perfectly private audit trail" / commitment-consistent encryption line).

## Decision
Ship the everlasting-privacy **primitive + binding proof** as a composable, independently-cross-verified companion artifact (`reference/src/everlasting.ts`), not a rewrite of the transcript:

- **A second NUMS generator H** (`group.ts`): `H = ristretto255_from_hash(SHA-512("vvp-everlasting-pedersen-H-v1"))` — the RFC 9496 one-way map over a 64-byte digest, so `dlog_G(H)` is unknown. It is pinned to a checked-in constant and **fails closed** if the derived value differs; the independent Python verifier derives the **byte-identical** H via libsodium's `crypto_core_ristretto255_from_hash` (verified equal; RFC 9380 is deliberately *not* used — libsodium does not expose it, so it would break cross-verification).
- **A perfectly-hiding Pedersen commitment** per candidate, `C = v·G + d·H`, with `d` uniform in `[0,N)`. With `d` uniform, `C` is uniform over the prime-order group independently of `v`, so the commitment trail `{C_i}` leaks **zero** information about any vote even to a computationally unbounded / quantum adversary.
- **A generalized-Schnorr consistency NIZK** (`proofs.ts` `proveConsistency`/`verifyConsistency`) proving knowledge of `(v,r,d)` with `a=r·G ∧ b=v·G+r·PK ∧ C=v·G+d·H`. A **single shared response `zv`** across the `b`- and `C`-equations is the entire cross-binding: it forces the *same* `v` in the ciphertext and the commitment. Verifier hardening mirrors the existing proofs: `inRange` on every response (reject non-canonical scalars ≥ N), the challenge is **recomputed** (never read from the proof), all points are parsed/validated, and the Fiat–Shamir challenge — domain `everlasting-consistency-v1` — binds `[G, H, PK, a, b, C, A_a, A_b, A_C]`. Combined with the existing disjunctive bit-proof on `(a,b)`, this establishes that `C` commits to a value in `{0,1}`.
- **Commitments are additively homomorphic**, so `ΣC_i` perfectly hides the tally while remaining bound (via the per-ballot proofs) to the count the ElGamal aggregate decrypts to.

Held to the project's bars: a TypeScript verifier and an independent Python/libsodium verifier (`verify_everlasting_trail`) cross-checked in CI on every push; a self-test battery (`selftest.ts` §24); and an adversarial review round (CRYPTO_REVIEW). The trail is decrypted **never** — `C`, `A_*`, and the responses are privacy artifacts and MUST NOT enter any tally path; the opening `Σd_i` is never published.

### The strongest claim that is true, and what we MUST NOT claim
**TRUE:** the commitment trail is **unconditionally (perfectly) hiding** and is cryptographically **bound** to the verifiable ElGamal ballots; the consistency NIZK is complete, special-sound, and HVZK (NIZK via Fiat–Shamir in the ROM) and leaks nothing about `(v,r,d)`, and does not weaken `C`'s perfect hiding.

**MUST NOT CLAIM:** (1) post-quantum / everlasting **integrity** — binding of `C` and soundness of the proof are discrete-log based; a quantum adversary who computes `dlog_G(H)` can equivocate a commitment, and one who computes `dlog_G(PK)` can decrypt. Integrity stays classical. (2) That the **current demo transcript is everlasting-private** — it co-publishes `(a,b)`, the bit proofs, and the decryption shares, all of which reveal `v` to a future curve-breaker; **as published the trail artifact is only computationally private.** (3) That the consistency proof alone proves bit-ness (it proves *same-v*; bit-ness comes from the bit-proof on `(a,b)`). (4) Unconditional *binding* — hiding is unconditional, binding is computational; no scheme is both. (5) RFC 9380 conformance — this is the RFC 9496 one-way map.

### Migration path to a fully everlasting-private deployment (deferred)
Full everlasting privacy of a *deployed* election requires the Cuvelier–Pereira–Peters discipline: the commitments `{C_i}` become the **permanent** public record, while the ElGamal ciphertexts, bit proofs, and decryption transcript are **ephemeral** threshold-tally material, destroyed after the aggregate is decrypted and never permanently published. **Hard gate:** once `(a,b)` and its bit-proof leave the permanent record, nothing constrains `v ∈ {0,1}` in the everlasting view, so a separate **everlasting / statistically-hiding bit/range argument directly on `C`** becomes mandatory (else ballot-stuffing soundness is lost in that view). A post-quantum *integrity* story (lattice-based commitments/arguments, or hybrid) is a separate, larger effort and is explicitly out of scope here.

## Consequences
GOOD: closes the one frontier privacy gap the project had not named; gives a sound, peer-reviewed, cross-verified primitive that a deployment can build everlasting privacy on; the perfect-hiding property is information-theoretic, not "trust our curve"; the honest scope is written into the code, the demo, this ADR, and the verifiers. BAD/COSTS: as shipped it is a *primitive*, not an everlasting-private election — the demo artifact publishes both layers and is only computationally private, which must be stated every time; it adds per-candidate commitment + proof bytes and verification cost; the migration to a real everlasting deployment needs the ephemeral-ciphertext operational model **and** an everlasting bit/range proof on `C` that is not yet built; and it is privacy-only — integrity remains classical-crypto and quantum-vulnerable. These are accepted: an honestly-scoped, sound primitive that names exactly what remains is the right increment, and far better than either silence on the harvest-now-decrypt-later threat or an over-claimed "post-quantum voting" banner.

## Alternatives considered
(1) **Do nothing / stay silent** — REJECTED: harvest-now-decrypt-later is a real, named threat for a decades-lived public board; silence reads as "unconsidered." (2) **Reuse `b = v·G + r·PK` as the commitment** (the CCE observation that `b` is "already a commitment to `v` under `PK`") — REJECTED: `b` is only *computationally* hiding and, worse, hiding under a key the trustees deliberately open to tally; it can never be an everlasting artifact. A separate `C` under an *unknown*-dlog `H` is precisely the upgrade from computational to information-theoretic hiding. (3) **A separate bit/range proof on `C` now** — REJECTED for the current both-layers-published module (bit-ness is inherited from the bit-proof on `(a,b)` + the consistency binding); **REQUIRED** at the CPP migration and recorded as a hard gate above. (4) **Claim post-quantum privacy of the transcript outright** — REJECTED as an over-claim: the co-published ciphertexts defeat it; we ship the primitive and the migration ADR instead. (5) **Full post-quantum integrity (lattice NIZKs) now** — REJECTED as out of scope and a much larger, separately-auditable effort; this ADR is privacy-only and says so.
